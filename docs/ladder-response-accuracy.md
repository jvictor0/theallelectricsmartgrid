## Which curve exists

Fix the knobs. Draw independent band-limited records at 192 kHz, each normalized to squared RMS 1/32 and rounded to binary32. Reset at a uniformly random position in the first record. At subsequent record boundaries keep the four ladder memories, ten detector memories, and sufficient analyzer samples and phase to finish any pending epoch. The next record and that state completely determine the next state and the measured powers. Give each edge its own 240 power readings. Two records that reach the same state can have different readings along the way. [Input and analyzer protocol](../scripts/ladder_noise_grid/README.md)

For one band, let Z be the limiting time-average power in a single run. The Lean theorem gives a function h of state with h(i) equal to the expected Z from initial state i. On almost every path, h stays at Z after a finite time. The actual band readings can fluctuate forever. The same full-probability event covers all bands and all possible initial boundary states. The finite first record contributes zero weight to the limiting average. With random initial phase, average h over the first boundary state as well. Divide the expected wet power by the positive expected dry power in each band, then take dB and apply the drawing convention. Averaging per-run dB would define another curve.

The proof writes each accumulated power as a sum of three parts. The first part averages h along the visited states. The second has conditionally zero increments and a fourth-moment bound that makes its running average vanish almost surely. The last part depends only on the first and last state and vanishes after division by the number of records. The expected sum of squared changes in h is bounded. Since h takes finitely many values, it eventually stops changing almost surely. Its total expected squared change from reset equals the variance of Z. A different reachable class can therefore matter to a single run. The target h(reset) is well defined even in that case. [Power, simultaneous spectrum and variance proofs](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/RunAverage.lean)

The dry denominator admits an explicit formula. At fixed epoch alignment the analyzer needs less than one record of past samples. Let D(a,b) be its power on record b preceded by a, including the FIR tail and pending epoch. With independent records of probabilities w, Lean proves that the observed dry average converges almost surely to ΣₐΣᵦ w(a)w(b)D(a,b). Averaging over alignment gives the dry denominator for the survey. The independent spectral enclosure for the normalized noise law bounds all 120 of those phase-averaged dry powers away from zero. The smallest lower endpoint exceeds 1.40 × 10⁻⁶ in analyzer power units. These denominators apply at every knob position. [Two-record theorem](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/RunAverage.lean) · [Per-band dry power bounds](../build/results/ladder-noise-grid/screen-v3/physical-stress/wet-power-certificate.json)

The coefficient check proves contraction of each detector SVF in the norm with squared value x²+xy+y², at radius 0.9999 per sample before rounding. The complete nonlinear ladder can self-oscillate. Its boundedness proof uses the clamped rational saturators, positive detector power floor, and the stability of the *detector* SVFs. It allows every value of K s³ in the survey. ## What would certify physical accuracy everywhere

An all-controls dB guarantee for the fresh-noise population still needs a quantitative bound on the difference between h and the finite measured powers, including variation between knob samples. Convergence establishes the target at every setting. The width certificate quantifies interpolation and compression against the archive. The refined sweep measures at additional control positions. None of these supplies a rate for a rare reachable response to contribute to the ensemble power. A useful bound on the expected wet-power residual over reachable states, or on the remaining contribution of histories after a finite run, would close that gap if made uniform within each relevant control cell. The drawing already has the proof that propagates such bounds through the floor. ## Decision for an app trial

The frozen fit has a statistical guarantee for the acquisition protocol. With 95% confidence, at least **96.32%** of fresh random stock-probe experiments have visible whole-curve error at most **0.569 dB**. Draw the drive and resonance knobs uniformly, cutoff uniformly in log Hz, reset, discard 4.096 seconds and measure the next 5.461 seconds. Use 16 noise records and average their linear powers. The guarantee includes the randomness of the control and measurement. The infinite-time ensemble error at each individual control remains open.

The check used 80 held-out groups after excluding all 16 groups used for model selection. In each held-out group two random controls shared the same 16 records. Take the larger error in each pair. The largest of these 80 scores was 0.568149 dB. Under independent group draws, the probability that their maximum covers less than a fraction p of future groups is at most p⁸⁰, including ties. For p = 0.9632 this is less than 0.05. Coverage of the pair implies at least the same coverage for a randomly selected member. As with any randomized audit here, the confidence interpretation models the pseudorandom groups as independent draws. ## Probability of reaching the long-run curve at fixed controls

Fix 249.70 Hz cutoff, 60.33% drive and 97.73% resonance. Of the three previously selected high-feedback challenges, this setting retained audio-history differences longest in the six-record check. The comparison curve was frozen before the calibration. It is the ratio of pooled wet and dry powers from the earlier 24 lag-corrected fresh-noise pairs at this setting, including the random initial record phase. Its distance from the infinite-time population is an unknown number η, in visible whole-curve RMS dB.

Run 72 new independent groups of 16 normalized digital records at exactly these controls. Each record starts at reset, discards the first 4.096 seconds and measures the next 5.461 seconds with the stock filter. Pool wet and dry powers separately within each group. The median distance of a group's curve from the fixed comparison curve was 0.1473 dB. The maximum was 0.183974 dB. The order-statistic argument now applies to randomness in the noise at these particular controls. Under the independent-draw interpretation, with at least 97.5% confidence at least 95% of future groups fall within **0.184 dB** of the comparison curve, since 0.95⁷² < 0.025. By the triangle inequality the bound against the physical long-run curve is **0.184 + η dB**. A 0.5 dB goal, for example, would leave 0.316 dB to certify. A deterministic certificate for η preserves the 97.5% calibration confidence. A second statistical certificate with at most 2.5% failure would give at least 95% joint confidence by the union bound. [Calibration results](../build/results/ladder-noise-grid/screen-v3/fixed-probe-250/summary.json) · [Coverage transfer proof](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/ProbeCoverage.lean)

One route to η uses two copies driven by the same fresh records, with one copy a record older. Use full-record band readings with the stationary protocol's epoch alignment. The measured survey's fixed-phase partial-window convention is already included in the independent comparison above. At age m, write aₘ for the expected linear power of the full-record readout. The expected signed difference of the copies at the next comparison is aₘ₊₁ − aₘ. Summing subsequent differences leaves aₘ₊ₖ − aₘ. The known long-run target is the limit of the averages of aₘ₊ₖ over k. Hence bounding all those signed sums encloses the target even if individual expected record readings oscillate.

For a complete pair state s, let d(s) be its expected older-minus-younger band power during the next record. P takes the expectation over the successor pair under a shared fresh record. Seek functions l ≤ 0 and u ≥ 0 such that

```
l(s) ≤ d(s) + (P l)(s)       d(s) + (P u)(s) ≤ u(s).
```

The two inequalities bound every finite accumulated signed difference by induction. If the pair at audit age m has distribution πₘ, the physical mean is between aₘ + Eπₘ[l] and aₘ + Eπₘ[u]. Lean checks this implication. The check only needs to cover pair states reachable at or after that age, with their full joint dynamics. It permits cancellations and does not assume that all histories eventually become identical. Candidate bounds can be sought by backing up l ← min(0, d + P l) and u ← max(0, d + P u) or by fitting remaining signed differences to joint audio and detector states. To certify a candidate one must verify the inequalities under the full fresh-record law and bound the finite expectations in the endpoints. Sampled transitions can guide this search; a rare long-lived pair can defeat a candidate. A narrow enough numerical certificate at 249.70 Hz remains open. The sampled comparison curve itself has finite-sample uncertainty, which must also be included or bypassed by enclosing the physical powers directly and comparing the enclosure to the frozen bytes. [Signed tail theorem](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/LagBudget.lean)

### Isolating the remaining wet-power error

For the specified randomly phased fresh normalized Gaussian records, the stationary dry spectrum can be tightly enclosed without running the ladder. For host length M and native length N = 4M, the unrounded single-record covariance is the sum over the M−2 allowed Fourier modes, each weighted by 1/[32(M−2)]. At lag d the two samples are in the same record with probability max(0, 1−|d|/N). Samples in separate records have zero covariance. Multiply by that probability, apply each FIR autocorrelation, decimate, multiply by the Hann autocorrelation, and take the cosine sum for each frequency bin. Overlapping windows have the same expectation. This preserves the actual analyzer's bin overlaps and two sample rates. [Noise law](ladder-wet-power-proof.md#input-law-and-reference-moments) · [Tapered-periodogram expectation](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article/111/4/1313/7703280)

It encloses the trigonometric and weighted sums with outward binary64 operations, then adds the effect of input rounding to binary32 via the FIR norm and Parseval. All 120 intervals lie inside the previous dry bounds. The greatest interval width is 0.000431 dB. Using their geometric midpoints costs at most 0.000216 dB anywhere after interpolation and the −60 dB clamp, for any wet spectrum. ### Working backward from the 0.5 dB claim

At 249.70 Hz the target is 95% confidence that at least 95% of fresh 16-record surveys are within 0.5 dB whole-curve RMS of the long-run curve. [Full conditional theorem](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/SurveyAccuracy.lean)

Fix one band, with frozen reference power W. Let Y be the mean of the four record powers measured at ages 4, 5, 6 and 7. The independent 1,024-history audit already bounds E[min(Y, 2W)] in all 120 bands at once. There are two differences between that quantity and long-run power.

1. **Power above the cap.** For every real y and positive W, the excess above 2W satisfies 4W max(0, y − 2W) ≤ (y − W)². When y exceeds 2W this is the inequality (y − 3W)² ≥ 0. Thus a bound of **0.12** on E[(Y/W − 1)²] would bound omitted power by **0.03W**. The maximum observed bandwise second moment was 0.0211 in the first audit and 0.0215 in the independent confirmation. These are sample means.
2. **Power attributable to the measurement age.** Let Δⱼ be the expected older-minus-younger band power when the two copies get the same next record, after j shared records. It equals the change in expected power from age j to age j+1. For each terminal age N ≥ 7, the correction to the age-4-through-7 mean is exactly Δ₄/4 + Δ₅/2 + 3Δ₆/4 + Σ₇≤ⱼ<ᴺ Δⱼ. If the absolute value of this expression stays within **0.03W**, the long-run correction lies in that interval too. This follows by averaging the finite identities over N. It works for real-valued dynamics whose expected powers have the specified long-run Cesàro mean. It allows continuing differences between states and continuing fluctuations in power. The proof shifts the fresh-noise stream while preserving the randomly selected initial phase and history. The 96 saved pairs give a maximum of **0.004742W** over all bands and finite terminal ages for their sample-mean correction. After the last observed agreement their sample sum stays constant. The population bound still needs a proof.
