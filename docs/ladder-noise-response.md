# Visible white-noise response of the Naive Reupping Ladder

The 9,996,763-byte fit reproduces the detector-enabled stock filter to **0.142 dB RMS** on 160 held-out settings. A certificate over the dense measured archive gives **0.19 dB RMS** averaged over all controls and log frequency, and **1.34 dB whole-curve RMS** at every control position. The exact digital long-run mean exists under stationary input. A physical bound for one continuous low-resonance box is 4.41 dB whole-curve RMS and 0.113 dB pointwise above 1.244 kHz, under the stationary normalized-record laws described below. A tight physical bound throughout control space remains open.

## What appears on screen

The sampled response computes ten times the base-ten logarithm of power, with a guard near −100 dB. The drawing code then sends every value at or below **−60 dB** to the same row. For raw gain in dB write `C(a) = max(−60, a)`. A vertical error of one dB equals plot height divided by 120. The fit, validation, integer certificate and curve comparison all use the final drawing clamp. Two hidden curves contribute exactly zero. When one is hidden, only the distance from the other curve to −60 counts. If a segment crosses the floor, the comparison splits it at the crossing. At fixed controls the largest difference between two such band curves occurs at a band knot, a boundary, or one of their floor crossings. Between adjacent such frequencies the difference is affine in log frequency. [Power conversion](../private/src/SampledTransferFunction.hpp) · [Drawn coordinates](../JUCE/SmartGridOne/Source/PathDrawer.hpp)

The filter starts from zero with fixed drive, resonance and cutoff. Its saturation, feedback delay and both envelope detectors participate. For each of 120 bands, average linear wet and matched dry powers separately, take their ratio, and convert to dB. Connect the raw band centers linearly on a log-frequency axis, extend flat to the two boundary edges, then apply the drawing clamp. Whole-curve RMS integrates the squared visible difference uniformly in log frequency, 20 Hz–20 kHz. The frequency resolution is that of the specified windowed, dual-rate 4,096-point analysis. The acquisition uses 48 kHz Gaussian records without DC or Nyquist, Fourier interpolation to 192 kHz, and 0.176777 RMS per record. [Survey protocol](../scripts/ladder_noise_grid/README.md)

## Certificate against the measured archive

The reference here is the continuous interpolant of the observed spectra. Every one of its 1,866,240 control cells and 121 frequency segments was checked. Interpolation in a cell is a convex combination of 16 vertices. Because a floor can cut a cell in its interior, simply clipping the vertices before interpolation gives the wrong crossing. For vertex levels relative to the floor between −v and u, the difference between the interpolated clipped value and clipping after interpolation lies in [0, uv/(u+v)]. For model and reference the two corrections both have the same sign, so their difference is bounded by the larger correction. Squaring the resulting visible difference and applying Jensen gives an upper bound from the vertices. Integer arithmetic accumulates it with rational upper bounds on segment widths. Two rounding allowances sum to 1/128 dB. Cells entirely below the floor in both curves have zero actual error.

The bound on the global RMS is **0.19 dB**. At an arbitrary setting the bound on whole-curve RMS is **1.34 dB**. The looser pointwise bound is **8.797 dB**, including frequencies between band centers. Thus a guarantee about the whole visible curve does not supply the same guarantee about the tip of a single peak. For straight-line rendering, an endpoint displacement bound also holds between endpoints. The extrema and sums were computed by the integer checker. Lean proves the inequalities for clipping, interpolation, path height and line segments, and checks the final rational totals. Python executes the integer scan and the numerical drawing tests. Lean checks their analytical bounds and final certificate arithmetic. [Integer checker](../build/results/ladder-noise-grid/screen-v3/certify.py) · [Archive and model hashes, sums and bounds](../build/results/ladder-noise-grid/screen-v3/certificate.json) · [Lean drawing lemmas](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/Display.lean)

The same scan records separate all-drive, all-resonance bounds for each of the 360 cutoff intervals. Taking the largest whole-curve RMS bound in each cutoff decade gives 1.34 dB for 20–200 Hz, 0.96 dB for 200 Hz–2 kHz, and 0.98 dB for 2–20 kHz. These bound the packed model against the survey interpolant. The physical interpolation error between measured cutoffs is a further term, as the stock runs below demonstrate. ## What the long-run response means

For the fresh-record noise law, Lean now proves almost-sure convergence of all wet and dry powers on a single stream. An exhaustive stock coefficient check and rounding bounds cover the whole surveyed control range. The peak-width certificate and new cutoff measurements quantify the remaining numerical accuracy question. [Long-run proof, peak widths and new midpoint comparisons](ladder-response-accuracy.md)

For each band the target is the **expected long-run wet power from reset**, divided by its matched expected long-run dry power, assumed positive. This gives a single ensemble curve at fixed knobs. Individual noise histories can give different limiting powers. Three constructions distinguish what can be proved for each noise law.

**Ongoing stationary digital noise.** Fix the knobs and a two-sided stationary law on valid digital input blocks. Assume the specified arithmetic stays finite on its reachable histories, so the measured band reward is bounded. The four stage memories and the ten detector memories form a finite state space. Include the finite analysis histories needed to evaluate each block. This also covers correlated, band-limited digital input when its law is stationary under the chosen block shift.

1. For a given input history let `Iₙ(t)` contain all states reachable at time t by playing the last n inputs from any initial state. The finite nonempty sets decrease with n and stabilize at a nonempty set `Cₜ`. The first stabilization depth `Dₜ` is measurable because stabilization is a countable intersection of equality tests of measurable finite sets.
2. The transition maps `Cₜ` onto `Cₜ₊₁`, so its cardinality can only decrease. Stationarity makes the two expected cardinalities equal, hence cardinalities agree almost surely at all integer times. The transition is a bijection between consecutive sets. Giving each member of `Cₜ` equal conditional weight over the driving path defines an invariant joint probability law.
3. Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem on that invariant law gives a time-average band power for almost every path and every state in its core. Each such state has positive conditional weight. Take the common full-measure event for all integer shifts and all bands.
4. From reset at zero, the state at n belongs to `Iₙ(n)`, and thus to `Cₙ` if `Dₙ ≤ n`. The probability of never entering is at most `Pr(Dₙ > n) = Pr(D₀ > n) → 0`. Once inside, the finite prefix has zero weight in the long-run average. Bounded convergence also gives convergence of the expected powers.

This proves almost-sure existence even with several recurrent responses. There is no rate in the depth argument. Lean checks stabilization, pushing the uniform core weights forward, and almost-sure eventual entry given the stationary depth distribution. The invariant-law construction, its Birkhoff step and the application to the stock state layout are the written part of this proof. This argument is also an instance of the finite-state channel ergodic theorem. [Lean finite-state lemmas](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/DigitalNoise.lean) · [Gray, Dunham and Gobbi](https://web.mit.edu/6.441/www/reading/IT-V33-N5_2.pdf)

**Finite-state noise generator or fresh independent finite digital records.** Include the noise-generator memory in the state. Let P take a function of the next state to its conditional mean given the current state. Let r be the expected band power during one block given that same current state. The long-run mean h and an auxiliary function v solve two linear equations,

```
P h = h
r   = h + v − P v
```

Lean proves these equations always have a solution for a finite row-stochastic P. The kernel and range of I−P intersect only at zero, since iterating a vector with a nonzero fixed increment would contradict boundedness of stochastic averaging. Rank-nullity gives the required decomposition of any reward. Summing the second equation over n blocks leaves only a boundary term. The expected mean from reset differs from h(reset) by at most `2 ‖v‖∞ / n`. Consequently h is uniquely determined even if v has several choices. Finite rational transition probabilities and digital rewards permit ordinary exact linear algebra. Fourteen binary32 feedback words alone allow up to 2⁴⁴⁸ bit patterns. Constructing the full matrix is impractical.

More practically, an approximate solution with a certified one-block power residual δ at every reachable state gives long-run error at most δ, provided h is harmonic. A constant proposed band mean is always harmonic. Choosing a good v is how a later certificate can account for transient energy. The composed Lean theorem takes that one-block residual and proves a bound on the actual long-run band’s displacement in plot coordinates, including silence and crossings. This is a quantitative proof obligation on *expected band power*. It can use coarse enclosures wherever the upper possible power already draws at the floor. [Lean existence and residual bound](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/MarkovPower.lean) · [Lean stationary-band-to-screen theorem](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/PopulationDisplay.lean)

**A repeated normalized noise record.** With a fixed digital record, sample the complete filter state at the same record boundary. Two bit-identical states prove that the entire future waveform repeats. There is a return within the number of different possible states. Replay one whole cycle, carrying the FIR tail across the boundary, to compute its long-run mean. The witness uses all 14 words. It also checks bitwise agreement with the stock processor on the first record and rechecks the boundary state after the complete cycle. Lean proves the bounded search and the equality between cycle mean and limiting mean. Periodic replay and fresh-noise streaming define different input ensembles. [Lean repeated-record theorem](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/RecordLimit.lean) · [Stock state extraction](../build/results/ladder-noise-grid/screen-v3/Cycle.cpp)

## Evidence about long runs

After freezing the fitted bytes, the blind audit drew 96 new drive/resonance/log-cutoff settings with 12 new normalized records each. The preselected pass criterion required all 12 exact returns within 16 repeats and at most 0.5 dB whole-curve error against the resulting pooled powers. **92/96 passed**. Three settings had an unresolved record and one completed setting measured 0.508 dB. The 93 complete settings gave 0.125 dB pooled RMS. With independent uniform settings and independent record banks, the one-sided 95% exact binomial lower limit for passing this experiment is 90.7%. This confidence statement includes the timeouts. It applies to the twelve-record test with fresh draws. [Blind audit results](../build/results/ladder-noise-grid/screen-v3/stationary.json) · [Audit protocol](../build/results/ladder-noise-grid/screen-v3/stationary_audit.py)

In the longer targeted study, 354/384 records reached exact returns. At 310.95 Hz and full resonance all 32 records returned, one needed 258 records to enter, and the longest period was 163 records. The pooled exact-cycle spectrum and fit differed by 0.169 dB RMS. At 20.99 Hz, just 2/32 records returned within 512 attempts per record. The finite-window stock fit at that setting is well measured, but the two exact returns cannot establish its population curve. Each record represents 9.557 seconds of audio. ## The remaining physical error bound

The fit-to-archive certificate, even over every cell, leaves the error between that archive and the infinite-noise population at unmeasured controls. The existence proof does not supply its size. A practical sufficient certificate is to bound the true wet band powers by lower and upper functions across each control cell, with a corresponding positive dry bound. Interpolate those envelopes at the spectral knots before clipping, so crossings are covered. If the upper envelope is below the floor the whole region is certified at zero displayed height. Otherwise compare the two clipped endpoints to the fit. The display lemma formalizes this monotone step. For a finite-state source the Poisson residual above gives one route to the physical power bounds. For compact real-valued models, the extremal finite-window construction bounds expected powers over all candidate histories and has convergent upper and lower endpoints. Useful numerical widths throughout the resonant region still have to be established. A bound on this physical term would complete a numerical math-to-drawing guarantee on the surveyed domain. [Lean finite-window enclosure](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/StationaryWindows.lean)

### An exact limitation of the frozen model

At fixed cutoff and drive, the nominal resonance affects processing through the capped base feedback. If two prepared base-feedback values agree, both detector updates, the compensated output and all subsequent samples agree on **every** shared input from reset. Lean checks the identity directly on the ladder step. It holds independent of noise, duration, and how quickly the controller settles. The stock implementation reads the same active fields and the witness was also checked for bit-identical output over 192,000 samples. [Cap theorem](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/ResonanceCap.lean) · [Stock arithmetic and waveform check](../build/results/ladder-noise-grid/screen-v3/physical-stress/stock-cap.json)

At 18,700.94 Hz and 90.8333% drive, 55.5556% and 62.5% resonance both produce exactly the same stored base feedback, 0.624023556709. The two **fitted** curves differ by more than 0.4027 dB whole-curve RMS. An integer/rational check reads the frozen coefficient arrays at these knob positions, integrates their squared difference exactly on each log-frequency segment, and conservatively encloses the final octave length. Both curves are visible throughout the band. The triangle inequality forces at least one fit to miss their common physical curve by **more than 0.2013 dB RMS** for any noise law for which that curve is defined. This is a lower bound on worst-case error and gives no lower bound on average error. Collapsing equivalent resonance settings when fitting could remove this discrepancy. For example, on a fixed physical plateau, replacing the family by the midpoint of any two of its displayed fits reduces the old worst *squared* error by at least one quarter of their squared separation. The Lean proof uses the parallelogram identity in the RMS norm. [Rational certificate](../build/results/ladder-noise-grid/screen-v3/physical-stress/cap-certificate.json) · [Checker](../scripts/ladder_noise_grid/response_fit/cap_certificate.py)

There is also a useful upper bound on what remains to prove along this axis. Consider drive from 65/72 to 1, cutoff from the 345th of 360 log-grid steps (14,997.88 Hz) to 20 kHz, and resonance from 57/72 to 1. The stock check covered all 3,496,733 binary32 cutoff values in the corresponding interval. A prepared gain of at least 3.73 and requested feedback of at least 1.68 suffice throughout it. The largest possible stock cap from the conservative arithmetic endpoints is 1.516. The physical waveform at each fixed cutoff and drive is therefore constant over these resonance settings. Over the same box, the frozen model departs from its full-resonance curve by at most **0.116 dB RMS** and **0.305 dB at any frequency**. This bound follows by convex interpolation from 512 packed control vertices. An absolute physical error certificate for the full-resonance face would extend to the entire box with that additional RMS allowance. The absolute error on the face remains open.

### A weaker condition for the physical proof

For a finite-state noise generator the reset proof can weight a bad state by how often the specified reset visits it. Let δ bound the one-block power residual after subtracting the change in the transient-energy potential. Allow an additional residual of up to M on a set B of states. If the expected long-run fraction of time in B from reset is p, then the error in expected long-run band power is at most **δ + M p**. Lean derives this from positivity and linearity of long-run expectations from reset and cancellation of the bounded potential. Several recurrent classes are allowed. To obtain a numerical certificate this way, the ladder would need verified residual and occupancy bounds over the knob cells in question. The matched cutoff refinements address a separate error between measurements. [Reset-occupancy proof](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/ResetOccupancy.lean)

## What the eightfold cutoff sweep can establish

The new GPU run fills 2,520 intermediate cutoff positions across the original 73 × 73 knob grid. The frozen 9,996,763-byte UI model is the first candidate. Each completed stage compares that same model with the refined finite-record reference and produces an error certificate for every cutoff interval. Reusing the noise bank of the left-hand parent vertex lets us compare neighboring cutoffs under paired noise. The seed-half and early/late diagnostics remain available to inspect sampling variability and settling. A new independent stock audit is appropriate if the additional data are used to select or train a replacement. [Acquisition protocol](../scripts/ladder_noise_grid/README.md)

For a numerical guarantee against the **physical** curve, let the signed error at a control setting be the difference between its true displayed curve and the fitted displayed curve. Take the norm over log frequency after applying the −60 dB drawing clamp to each curve. Suppose the measurements at the eight corners of a control cell have physical error at most B, and the fit differs from those measurements by at most E. Suppose also that along axis i the signed error changes by at most Jᵢ + Lᵢ times the distance moved. The jump Jᵢ can cover finite-precision discontinuities. Lean proves the whole-cell error is at most

```
B + E + Σᵢ (Jᵢ + Lᵢ hᵢ / 2)
```

Here hᵢ is the cell width on that axis. Each cell may use different constants. Reducing the log-cutoff width eightfold reduces its slope allowance eightfold at the same L. A separate lemma gives M h² / 8 on a cutoff interval when both endpoint Taylor remainders obey a common second-order bound about the query. Floor crossings can use the slope bound. The checker quantifies the measured fit error E. Bounds on B and on the physical variation throughout a cell still require evidence about the dynamics beyond the sampled vertices. The experiment can expose failures of proposed tolerances and guide which cells need that work. [Lean control-cell and cutoff bounds](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/CutoffGrid.lean)

## First physical enclosure and the high-resonance case

A calculation independent of the wet measurements compares the detector-on waveform with a four-pole linear reference driven by the same input. In the cutoff interval 92.83–100.24 Hz, drive 0–1.389% and resonance 0–0.006945%, the binary32 audio filter's long-run physical curve is within **4.41 dB whole-curve RMS** of the frozen fit. Its physical ordinate lies at the display floor above the 1,243.561 Hz band center. On that tail the model error is at most **0.113 dB**. The noise law concatenates independent normalized 9.557-second Fourier records at a stationary random starting phase. The enclosure also covers repeatedly playing a random record. [Physical proof, scope and checker](ladder-wet-power-proof.md)

The waveform comparison takes absolute gains around the delayed feedback loop. It needs K s³ < 1, where K is the prepared base feedback and s the normalized saturation slope. At the hard stock settings near 250, 515 and 359 Hz those products are approximately 15.53, 29.38 and 7.11. There is a separate result about the controller at those settings. Write q and p for wet and dry detector power, F for the floor, R for the reference power gain, D for damping scale, and a for the common EMA rate. In exact real arithmetic, k is a Lipschitz function of log((q+RF)/(p+F)). Its Lipschitz constant is K M where M = 1/[4(1−DR)] if DR ≤ ½ and M = DR otherwise. Each shifted power can fall only by a factor 1−a in one sample. Summing positive and negative log increments gives a long-run upper bound on mean absolute feedback change of **4 K M (−log(1−a))** when the shifted powers stay bounded. F enters the finite endpoint correction and drops out of the limiting allowance. Lean checks the finite sum, the detector's EMA inequality and both slope cases. The upper bounds using the three reported parameter settings and stock coefficients in the real controller are respectively 0.001011, 0.001734 and 0.001186 feedback units per engine sample. A slowly changing feedback can still produce a large resonant peak. Bounding the wet power at those settings remains open. [Controller variation proof](../formal/DSP/Filters/NaiveReuppingLadder/NoiseResponse/DetectorVariation.lean)
