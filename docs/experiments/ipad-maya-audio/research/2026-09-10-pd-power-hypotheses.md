# iPad thermal state, hub power, and MAYA44 USB+ failures

Research date: 2026-09-10. Scope: manufacturer/Apple/USB sources and existing captures only. No live device query, setting change, hardware manipulation, or purchase was performed.

## Finding

**Thermally reduced iPad system demand could plausibly make this shared hub/power arrangement more stable, even at 100% battery. The mechanism is not demonstrated.** The narrower story that battery charging was active and then stopped exactly when audio improved is poorly supported by the current archive evidence.

The strongest direct correction to the previous assumption is already in the saved 12:54:31 PDT capture: battery 100%, `IsCharging=false`, `BatteryPower=0`, yet `SystemVoltageIn=14705`, `SystemCurrentIn=814`, and `SystemPowerIn=SystemLoad=11969`. With conventional mV/mA/mW scaling, 14.705 × 0.814 = 11.97 W was entering the iPad for system use. These are privately named telemetry fields, internally consistent but not calibrated, and this snapshot preceded the later hot/cooled runs. It does not measure hub output power, MAYA's 5 V, or any fault-time transient.

The power hypothesis deserves a short discriminating test; it does not currently outrank every host scheduling, USB transaction translator, audio-session, or device-specific interaction. The 21 transaction-error/restart dropouts and the 29 periodic processing-overrun holes must remain separate outcomes.

## Power flow and the thermal hypothesis

For a stable charger-powered arrangement, a useful conceptual balance is:

`charger input power ≈ iPad external input + MAYA + WRLD.BLDR + hub circuitry/conversion/cable losses`.

Inside the iPad, external input can run the screen, CPU/GPU, memory, radios, and audio system while net battery charging is zero. A USB host can consume external power while remaining data host: power role and data function are not synonymous. USB-IF describes adjustable system allocation and a host consuming power from an accessory. [USB-IF Power Delivery overview](https://www.usb.org/usb-charger-pd)

Given the observed iPad input near 15 V and the USB-powered peripherals' 5 V supply, it is reasonable to infer that the hub arrangement distributes and converts charger power to the peripherals while powering the iPad. The exact shared regulator, intermediate rail, capacitance, protection grouping, and thermal coupling are unknown. This missing circuit information is the essential gap between a plausible shared-power story and a demonstrated cause.

Apple says iPad charging stops when full and resumes below 95%; current tapers near full. The M3 Air supports an 80% limit, which stops filling near 80% and resumes near 75%, with occasional calibration charges to 100%. Therefore, a displayed 100% neither proves current battery filling nor establishes zero external demand. The 80% setting is a battery-filling control, not a documented disconnection of external system power. [Apple: charging and maintaining the iPad battery](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118418)

Apple also documents that heat can slow/stop charging, dim the display, reduce frame rates, and increase processing times. This supports both a potential reduction in electrical demand and a separate change in software/graphics scheduling. Apple does not publish a rule that this iPad's `serious` thermal state means a particular wattage, clock speed, battery-charging state, or USB PD renegotiation. [Apple: iPhone/iPad temperature management](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118431)

Three distinct mechanisms should not be collapsed:

1. **Lower sustained system load improves margin.** Thermal management reduces iPad input draw or rapid workload changes; a marginal charger/cable/hub power path becomes more stable or eventually cooler; MAYA transport errors become less frequent. Plausible, but requires measured demand reduction and evidence of coupling to the peripheral supply. Charger label wattage alone does not test this.
2. **A load transition itself disturbs regulation.** Load increases or decreases can produce supply undershoot or overshoot; a marginal path could briefly disturb MAYA or hub logic. TI documents this general converter behavior and its dependence on current-step size/slew, output components, and control response. This is engineering plausibility, not evidence that the Satechi contains TI parts or has a deficient rail. A one-time thermal load step would predict transition-local errors more directly than minutes of cleaner audio afterward. [TI: load transient response](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/lit/html/SSZT807/GUID-6956A641-46D5-4570-B319-AD09A74A77DD)
3. **PD contract or power-source change disturbs the hub.** A changed contract, hard reset, or source handoff could interrupt power/data. However, simply drawing less current within an existing contract does not establish that a contract changed. The selected capability is a ceiling, not mandatory consumption. The present logs do not demonstrate repeated renegotiation or role swaps during the spontaneous errors. TI's host/hub example specifically explains why removing a charger can interrupt peripheral data during a power-role handoff and why Fast Role Swap exists. It does not prove this exact iPad/hub pairing supports uninterrupted swapping. [TI: charger removal and Fast Role Swap](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/lit/html/SSZTAQ4/GUID-6E76B6C8-91AB-4C9B-99CC-BC7BB49B2D94)

A lower demand need not always improve a faulty system: it may cause a transition disturbance or put a converter into a different operating regime. Without a circuit or waveforms, invoking that possibility after every contrary observation makes the hypothesis unfalsifiable. Predeclare which version is being tested.

Satechi says its aluminum enclosure dissipates internal heat and warmth is expected with peripheral and charging activity. Touch alone does not establish overheating. Cooling the iPad does not measure or necessarily cool the hub; warming the iPad could reduce its demand while the hub follows a different temperature history. [Satechi heat/connectivity guidance](https://support.satechi.com/hc/en-us/articles/43165667988251-Performance-Connectivity-Issues-Connectivity-Expansion-FAQs)

## Fit to existing observations

- The latest cooled run had 21 approximately 225 ms losses with MAYA endpoint `0x82` transaction errors, restart activity, and callback stalls. Eighteen occurred in the first roughly 8m16 at nominal thermal state, three in the last roughly 7m32 at serious. This is compatible with improvement after thermal management but is one ordered, nonstationary session. Bursts, session history, and warm-up can produce the same association. A transport error is not a voltage measurement.
- The earlier hot 16-minute and 5-minute clean intervals, alongside two hot startup failures, show that hot/serious is not a guarantee of a healthy session. They are compatible with a steady-state stabilizing effect plus startup sensitivity, but also with audio-session initialization or controller state.
- A separate periodic burst had 29 holes spanning approximately 0.608 s, with onsets about 21.3 ms apart and 29 alternate-processing-budget overruns without matching USB errors/xrun-count changes. Supply explanations for the 225 ms transaction failures do not automatically explain those holes.
- Root-agent archive audit, received during this research: cooled run logged private SMC `Not charging:100` at 16:50:21.683 and `Not charging:110` at 16:55:08.683, before serious began at 16:57:27.165; battery remained 100%. Hot quiet comparison also logged both values (16:14:05 and 16:14:50). Their numeric meanings are undocumented here. This weakens a simple active-battery-fill-to-off story at the serious transition; do not decode 100/110 as percentages, temperatures, or named reasons.
- Root-agent preliminary control audit: SonoBus startup at 13:25 recorded `BatteryIsCharging=true` at 100%. This shows why the 12:54 snapshot cannot be propagated to every run. It is not yet evidence of a particular current or continuous charging through that control.
- Apparently clean SonoBus, Drambo, and desktop controls reduce the appeal of an unconditional hardware failure, but different system demand, duration, thermal histories, USB host/controllers, buffer/channel/endpoint activity, and initialization remain possible confounders. Exact control thermal matching is outside this bounded report and was being checked separately.

Existing local sources inspected:

The capture's adapter menu selects 15 V/2.27 A, approximately 34 W. It also advertises other voltage/current entries. None are measured actual consumption, nor the charger's 90 W label, nor the simultaneous 5 V peripheral budget. `ExternalConnected` and `ExternalChargeCapable` identify external power availability; `IsCharging`, net battery current, and `BatteryPower` address a different part of the power flow. The contradictory `FullyCharged=false` should remain raw.

Existing logs can establish logged charging-state transitions, reported thermal state, connection/route changes, hub enumeration, explicit PD/VBUS/overcurrent messages when present, and their temporal relationships to USB errors. The UI-off archive's 12:38:12 `Charging Completed`, VBUS 1, battery 100% is a point event. Absence of PD or overcurrent messages in a scoped query is not a high-speed electrical negative result. The archive decoder's wall-clock-adjustment warning further limits exact cross-stream correlation without monotonic alignment.

Battery temperature belongs to the iPad battery/power-source object. It is not hub or MAYA temperature. Lifetime thermal-limiting counters without per-run baselines do not establish a particular run was charge-limited. USB `bMaxPower`, self-powered bits, or OS power allocations, if present elsewhere, describe declared capability/requirements and cannot replace current measurement. USB-IF explicitly notes that legacy bus/self-powered classifications alone are inadequate for modern Type-C/PD hubs. A hub can support both upstream and external power. [USB-IF UFP-powered-hub white paper, §§1.3–1.4 and 2.1](https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/USB_Upstream_Facing_Port_Powered_Hub_White_Paper_0.9.pdf)

The decisive missing telemetry is synchronized: (a) iPad external V/I over time, (b) charger-to-hub V/I and PD messages, and especially (c) MAYA-end 5 V voltage/current during USB faults. A basic inline USB power meter can establish gross wattage changes or slow contract/voltage changes; its update interval may miss a short rail disturbance. A sufficiently fast voltage capture at MAYA is needed to reject short droop/overshoot, and a PD protocol trace to distinguish renegotiation/reset from ordinary load variation. Measurement adapters also change cable resistance/capacitance and sometimes negotiation, so instrumented and uninstrumented baselines are necessary. These are measurement requirements, not purchase recommendations.

## Minimal discriminating experiments, ranked

No experiment below was run. Keep build, patch, MAYA/WB data cables and ports, sample rate 48 kHz, 512-frame request, app/UI behavior, radios, and recording method fixed unless that item is the explicitly tested variable. Count USB transaction/restart losses and periodic processing holes separately. Exclude connection/reset settling windows and verify the intended audio route/rate before scoring. Use repeated, order-balanced intervals comparable to the existing 16-minute runs; a single clean short interval is weak evidence given the bursts.

| Rank | Comparison | Prediction that would support the hypothesis | Result that weakens it / interpretive limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Same hub, PD present versus absent, preferably establish each condition before recording. Start with similar battery/thermal state. | Repeated spontaneous transaction failures with PD present and substantial improvement with PD absent supports a charger/pass-through-dependent interaction. Returning PD restores the fault rate. | Similar failures in both conditions weaken an exclusively PD-fed explanation. PD absent may have insufficient available bus power; nonoperation is an unavailable test, not proof of the original cause. Source direction, regulator path, charger coupling/ground reference, host policy, and possibly USB session all change. A fault during unplugging is a transition effect, not the target steady-state outcome. |
| 2 | PD unchanged and continuous audio session; apply airflow only to the hub, alternating with no airflow and preferably measuring case temperature. | Errors follow hub temperature reproducibly while iPad thermal/load conditions remain comparable. This targets the hub-heat variant with few electrical changes. | Stable hub cooling with persistent errors weakens hub thermal sensitivity in the tested range. No measured case-temperature change makes a negative trial weak; case temperature is not junction temperature. Airflow reaching iPad/MAYA introduces another variable. |
| 3 | Independently power MAYA's USB supply using an already-available, supported topology; otherwise replace Satechi with a separately powered hub directly connected to iPad and keep iPad on battery. | Original errors disappear when MAYA no longer shares the suspect supply and return in the original arrangement. Strongest if data route and controller can stay identical and the independence is verified. | A replacement hub changes USB controller/transaction translator, firmware, cable path and power together: improvement implicates the original path but does not identify power alone. MAYA has no documented separate DC inlet, so “just power MAYA separately” is not a concrete option without suitable equipment. Do not improvise a passive dual-source USB cable. |
| 4 | Within a continuous session with PD fixed, vary iPad cooling/ordinary workload to obtain repeatable nominal/serious periods; leave hub temperature and location controlled. Prefer observing natural warm-up and cooling over forcing excess heat. | Measured iPad input power drops, error rate falls afterward, and both reverse on cooling; repeating this with independently supplied peripherals tests whether power sharing is necessary. | Thermal improvement without an input-power decrease weakens the demand mechanism. Same thermal relationship after effective power separation points toward host scheduling/USB policy. Workload changes alter scheduling directly, so the thermal label alone cannot establish the electrical path. |

Important model-specific topology limit: Satechi's product FAQ says ST-H4CPDM does not support cascading/daisy-chaining hubs. Therefore, a powered hub nested beneath this Satechi should not be presented as a manufacturer-supported clean isolation experiment. Direct replacement is operationally clearer but has the controller confound above. [Satechi product FAQ](https://satechi.com/products/4-port-usb-c-hub-with-pd)

The next useful outcome is reproducible dependence on PD presence or targeted hub temperature. If that is found, synchronized power/PD measurements can distinguish steady load, transients, and negotiation. Without that, the present evidence supports “possible shared-power interaction,” not “the charger is starving MAYA,” “the hub overheats,” or “thermal throttling fixes USB.”
